## Executive Summary

This project presents a fundamental equity research and valuation of **Ansell Limited (ASX: ANN)**, a global manufacturer and distributor of personal protective equipment and safety solutions serving healthcare, industrial, and specialized end markets.

The investment thesis is that the market may be underestimating the durability of Ansell's earnings recovery.

Following the normalization of post-pandemic demand, Ansell has demonstrated improving operating performance through Healthcare recovery, Industrial earnings resilience, manufacturing productivity, strategic pricing, and improved cash conversion.

The analysis evaluates whether these improvements represent a sustainable earnings trajectory rather than a temporary cyclical recovery.

Using a combination of discounted cash flow (DCF) valuation and adjusted market-based comparable valuation, the research arrives at a blended 12-month target price of **A$63.46**, representing an implied upside of **38.2%** against the A$45.91 reference price.

## Investment Thesis

The LONG recommendation rests on three principal arguments.

### 1. Healthcare Recovery

Ansell's Healthcare segment represents a significant opportunity for earnings recovery.

FY26 performance demonstrated:
- Revenue of US$1,192.9 million.
- Adjusted EBIT of US$170.7 million.
- Organic constant-currency revenue growth of 6.4%.
- Organic constant-currency adjusted EBIT growth of 23.5%.
- Adjusted EBIT margin improvement to 14.3%.

The investment case considers the potential for continued demand normalization, favorable product mix, and operational improvements to support segment profitability.

### 2. Industrial Earnings Resilience

The Industrial segment provides a complementary earnings base, supported by diversified end markets and specialized protective equipment demand.

FY26 performance demonstrated:
- Revenue of US$947.3 million.
- Adjusted EBIT of US$170.6 million.
- Organic constant-currency revenue growth of 3.3%.
- Organic constant-currency adjusted EBIT growth of 7.9%.
- Adjusted EBIT margin of 18.0%.

The segment's profitability and exposure to specialized applications support the argument that Ansell's earnings recovery is not exclusively dependent on Healthcare.

### 3. Improving Earnings Quality and Cash Generation

Ansell's earnings growth has outpaced revenue growth.

FY26 group performance included:
- Revenue of US$2,140.2 million.
- Adjusted EBIT of US$321.9 million.
- Adjusted EBIT growth of 14.1%.
- Adjusted EBIT margin of 15.0%, compared with 14.1% in FY25.
- Adjusted EPS of US148.6 cents.
- Operating cash flow of US$388.6 million.
- Capital expenditure of approximately US$49.0 million.
- Operating cash flow less capital expenditure of US$339.6 million.

This provides evidence of improved operating leverage, productivity, and cash generation.

## Valuation Framework

The valuation employs two complementary approaches to estimate intrinsic and market-relative equity value.

| Methodology | Value per Share | Weight |
|---|---:|---:|
| Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) | A$66.44 | 80% |
| Adjusted Market Comparable (MSA) | A$51.53 | 20% |
| **Blended Target Price** | **A$63.46** | **100%** |

### 1. Discounted Cash Flow Valuation

The primary valuation methodology is a five-year unlevered discounted cash flow model.

Forecast period: FY27–FY31.

Free cash flow to the firm is calculated as:

FCFF = NOPAT + D&A − CapEx − Change in NWC

The model incorporates:
- Segment-level revenue forecasts.
- Independent Healthcare and Industrial operating assumptions.
- Corporate cost projections.
- Segment margin expansion assumptions.
- Working capital requirements based on incremental revenue.
- Capital expenditure and depreciation forecasts.
- Weighted average cost of capital.
- Terminal value assumptions.

Base-case operating assumptions include:

| Assumption | Healthcare | Industrial |
|---|---:|---:|
| Annual revenue growth | 5.0% | 3.5% |
| Annual segment margin improvement | 15 bps | 15 bps |

Working capital investment is modeled at 28% of incremental revenue in the base case.

The model also evaluates alternative working capital assumptions of 24% and 33%.

Importantly, productivity improvements and KBU synergies are not separately added to EBIT after being incorporated into the operating margin assumptions. This avoids double counting.

**Base-case DCF valuation: A$66.44 per share.**

### 2. Adjusted Market Comparable Valuation

The secondary valuation uses MSA Safety as a market reference.

The comparable methodology considers:
- Enterprise value to EBITDA.
- MSA's observed valuation multiple.
- A 15% analytical discount to the observed multiple.
- Ansell's projected FY27 EBITDA.
- Net debt and share count assumptions.
- AUD/USD currency conversion.

The observed MSA EV/EBITDA multiple was approximately 14.77x at the November 20, 2026 reference date.

The discount reflects differences in business characteristics and is an analyst judgment rather than a directly observable market adjustment.

**Adjusted comparable valuation: A$51.53 per share.**

### 3. Reverse DCF and Scenario Analysis

The research also evaluates the expectations embedded in the prevailing share price.

Under the downside-case explicit cash-flow assumptions, the reverse DCF implies approximately 1.96% perpetual terminal growth, compared with the 2.50% base-case assumption.

This provides an additional framework for assessing the relationship between market expectations and modeled long-term cash-flow performance.

The reverse DCF is conditional on its specified operating assumptions and discount rate; it does not establish the market's actual internal forecast.

## Research Methodology

The project follows a fundamental, bottom-up investment research process.

**Business Analysis**
- Segment-level operating performance.
- Revenue composition and growth drivers.
- Earnings quality.
- Productivity and operating leverage.
- Strategic initiatives and acquisition synergies.

**Financial Analysis**
- Historical financial statement analysis.
- Revenue and earnings growth decomposition.
- Profitability and margin analysis.
- Cash flow and working capital assessment.
- Capital expenditure and cash conversion.

**Macroeconomic and Industry Analysis**
- External operating conditions.
- Healthcare and industrial demand transmission.
- Pricing and sourcing dynamics.
- Foreign exchange exposure.
- Supply chain and operating cost considerations.

**Equity Valuation**
- Five-year DCF forecasting.
- Market-based comparable valuation.
- Sensitivity and scenario analysis.
- Reverse DCF.
- Blended target price construction.

The objective is not simply to derive a target price, but to establish the operating and financial conditions necessary to justify it.

## Key Risks and Considerations

The investment thesis remains subject to several uncertainties:

- **Healthcare normalization:** Recovery may prove weaker or slower than forecast.
- **Industrial demand:** Industrial end-market weakness could constrain segment growth.
- **Margin sustainability:** Productivity improvements and favorable product mix may not persist.
- **Foreign exchange:** Currency movements and hedging costs can affect reported earnings.
- **Working capital:** Higher inventory or receivables requirements could weaken free cash flow.
- **Valuation sensitivity:** Terminal growth, discount rates, and comparable multiples materially influence equity value.
- **Execution risk:** Expected operating efficiencies and acquisition synergies may not be fully realized.

The 38.2% implied upside is a model-derived valuation outcome, not a guaranteed investment return.
